CPPS vs. CPHQ: Which Certification Should You Get?

A side-by-side look at the two most common credentials in healthcare quality and safety, and how to pick the right one first.

5 min read • September 2026

In this article

  1. The core difference
  2. Side-by-side comparison
  3. How the content differs
  4. How to choose
  5. Should you get both?

If you work in healthcare quality or safety, you've probably seen both CPPS and CPHQ in job postings, sometimes in the same one. They overlap, but they aren't interchangeable. Each one is built around a different version of the job.

The core difference

CPPS (Certified Professional in Patient Safety) is centered on harm: finding risk, recognizing it, investigating events, and responding in ways that prevent a repeat. It also treats harm to staff, such as workplace violence and psychological safety, as core content.

CPHQ (Certified Professional in Healthcare Quality) is centered on improvement and data: running improvement projects, building measures, analyzing performance, and leading quality programs. Patient safety is part of it, but as one area among several.

A simple test: if your week is full of event reports, root cause analyses and safety huddles, CPPS describes your job. If it's full of dashboards, measure specifications and improvement project charters, CPHQ does.

Both exams set their passing standard with the Angoff method, where expert panels judge each question, and both use statistical equating so different exam versions are fair. Neither is graded on a curve.

How the content differs

Where CPPS goes deeper

  • Event severity, escalation and reporting of serious events
  • Recognizing harm in high-risk areas: diagnosis, medications, procedures, deterioration, infections
  • Just culture and how leaders respond after an event
  • Human factors, SEIPS, Safety-II and high reliability
  • Workforce safety, including violence, incivility and support for staff after adverse events
  • Communication-and-resolution programs after harm

Where CPHQ goes deeper

  • Quality program leadership and strategy
  • Health data analytics: data design, collection, statistics and reporting
  • Performance and process improvement methods
  • Regulatory and accreditation requirements

Where they overlap

Both cover improvement models like PDSA, measurement types, run and control charts, root cause analysis and FMEA. If you prepare for one, you'll have a head start on those topics for the other.

How to choose

Choose CPPS first if you:

  • Hold or want a patient safety officer, specialist or manager role
  • Lead or facilitate RCAs and proactive risk assessments
  • Are a clinician moving into safety leadership
  • Meet the degree-plus-experience eligibility requirement

Choose CPHQ first if you:

  • Work as a quality manager, analyst or improvement advisor
  • Spend most of your time on measures, data and reporting
  • Don't yet meet CPPS eligibility (CPHQ has no formal requirement)

When in doubt, pull five job postings for the role you want next and count which credential appears more often. Employers tell you what they value.

Should you get both?

Many people in combined "quality and patient safety" roles eventually do. If you plan to, start with the one closest to your current job, since your daily work doubles as study time. Then take the second while the overlapping material (measurement, improvement methods, RCA) is still fresh.

Budget for renewal too. The two credentials run on different cycles and CE requirements, and CE credits that fit both content outlines can often support both, so track them carefully.
